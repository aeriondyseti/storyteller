## Premise

Varrow is a port city that has had no king for eleven years. The last one,
Edric the Fourth, died without an heir, and the Concord of the Five Houses has
ruled since in his name, under a crown that sits empty on a cushion in the
Customs House. In nine days the Houses meet for the Crown Vote, which happens
once a decade and decides who holds the Concord's seal for the next ten years.
Everyone who matters in the city is buying, selling or hiding something until
then.

Corwin Hale left Varrow six years ago with a customs warrant on his name and
has come back on a grain barge, before dawn, carrying a letter his dying mentor
made him swear to put into one pair of hands: Aldous Wren's, the Harbourmaster's
archivist. Corwin has not opened it. Somebody already knows he is here.

The tone is low-fantasy intrigue: no wizards, no prophecy that works, plenty of
superstition. Danger is mostly people, money and the sea. Violence is quick,
ugly and has consequences that last.

## Standing rules

- No working magic. Charms, saints and omens exist because people believe in
  them; none of them do anything the fiction cannot explain.
- Money matters. Bribes, debts and prices are specific and remembered.
- The Houses do not act in the open. Their people do.
- Nobody is simply good. Corwin's allies have their own reasons.
