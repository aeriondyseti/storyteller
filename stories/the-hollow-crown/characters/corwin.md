## Appearance

Thirty-four, lean in the way of a man who has eaten irregularly for six years.
Dark hair worn too long, a grey streak at the left temple he did not have when
he left. Ink stains he cannot scrub out of the first two fingers of his right
hand. A pale scar across the back of that hand, old. Dresses like a clerk who
has slept in his clothes, which he has: a brown wool coat with the customs
badge unpicked from the lapel, the stitches still visible.

## Background

Corwin was a tidewriter in the Customs House: one of the clerks who seal ships'
manifests and check them against what comes off the boats. He was good at it,
which is to say he noticed when the numbers lied. Six years ago he noticed the
wrong numbers, a House's grain tallies that did not match the barges, and a
warrant was sworn against him for theft of the seals before he could tell
anyone. He ran.

He spent the years since upriver in Hollin, keeping the books of an old
printer and pamphleteer, Tobias Marsh, who taught him that a fact on paper is
only as safe as the hands it passes through. Tobias died three weeks ago. Before
he died he gave Corwin a sealed letter and one instruction: into Aldous Wren's
hands, nobody else's.

## What he carries

The letter, sealed in green wax with Tobias's printer's mark, sewn into the
lining of his coat. Eleven silver crowns and some copper. A folding knife he is
not very good with. His old tidewriter's seal, which he was supposed to have
surrendered and did not.

## Ties

Mira Tessaly, who ran the chandlery below his old lodgings and once hid him for
a night. He left without paying back what she lent him to get out.
