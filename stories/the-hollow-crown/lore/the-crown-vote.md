King Edric the Fourth died eleven years ago of a fever, aged fifty, with no
acknowledged child. His crown was placed on a cushion in the Customs House "to
be held in trust until a lawful heir is proven", and the Concord of the Five
Houses has ruled in its name since. Varrow calls it the Hollow Crown, and the
phrase has come to mean any authority with nobody inside it.

Once every ten years the heads of the Five Houses meet in the Long Room for the
Crown Vote. Whichever House wins three votes holds the Concord Seal for the
next decade: the right to set harbour dues, appoint the Harbourmaster, and
command the Lamplighters. House Ashcombe has held it since Edric died and is
expected to again.

The charter that created the Concord has one clause everyone knows and nobody
discusses: if a lawful heir of Edric is proven before the Long Room, the vote
is void and the Concord dissolves. "Proven" means by the record: a sealed
entry in the Harbourmaster's archive.

The vote is nine days away at the start of the story.
