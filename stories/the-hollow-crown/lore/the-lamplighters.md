The night watch of Varrow, a chartered guild that lights the city's lamps and
enforces the curfew bell from second dusk to dawn. They answer to whichever
House holds the Concord Seal, which for eleven years has meant Ashcombe.

They wear grey oilcloth and carry hooked lamp-poles that double as weapons,
and a brass lantern on a chain. A lantern held up to your face means stop; a
lantern lowered means you have paid. Most streets pay. The Tallow Stair pays
the sergeant of the fish-dock patrol, Hesketh Crane, two crowns a week in
candles and coin.

They confiscate "unsealed" cargo and are supposed to deliver it to the Customs
House. Much of it never arrives. Arrests after curfew go to the Lamp Hall
cells, and nobody leaves the Lamp Hall before a magistrate sees them, which
can take a week.

They hold a copy of every open warrant, Corwin Hale's among them.
