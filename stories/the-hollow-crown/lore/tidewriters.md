Tidewriters are the clerks of the Customs House. They board incoming ships,
check cargo against the manifest, and seal the manifest with a numbered brass
seal; the sealed copy goes to the archive, and goods may not leave the docks
without it. A manifest bearing a seal is called a tidewrit and is good as law
in any court in Varrow.

Each seal is unique, numbered, and recorded in the seal inventory, signed
every quarter by the archivist. Losing a seal is dismissal; misusing one is
hanging, in theory, and a long stay in the Lamp Hall in practice.

A tidewriter's seal can also open the archive's lower door: the old privilege
of clerks who file their own manifests. Few remember that the lower door still
takes the seals. Aldous Wren does.

Corwin's seal is number forty-one. The inventory has listed it as surrendered
for six years.
